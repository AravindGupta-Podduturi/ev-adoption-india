Electric-vehicle (EV) adoption in India is highly uneven across states, yet most forecasts treat it as one series and say little about why states differ. This paper builds a two-layer analytics pipeline on a state--month panel (Delhi, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal; January 2022--September 2026) derived from Vahan registrations and combined with air-quality, fuel-price, weather, charging-infrastructure and per-capita GSDP data. The target is the EV adoption rate, the EV share of new registrations. An explanatory layer (tree models with SHAP, cross-checked by fixed-effects regression) ranks drivers; a forecasting layer benchmarks naive, ETS, SARIMA, Ridge, LightGBM and XGBoost models and a pre-declared top-3 ensemble, with every covariate aligned to its assumed publication date. Over 480 rolling-origin forecasts the pre-declared ensemble reaches an MAE of 1.94 percentage points (pp) against 2.34 for a last-value naive forecast, but is statistically tied with per-state SARIMA (1.93). In a harder single-origin test (train to March 2026, forecast April--September 2026) it does \emph{not} beat the naive forecast (2.41 vs 1.70 pp), because a 2026 level shift was missed. No driver passes our robustness test, and adding covariates raises forecast error. We report planning ranges for October 2026--March 2027, the data limits, and the exploratory (post hoc) models separately from the pre-declared one.
